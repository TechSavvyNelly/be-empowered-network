Guide

How to find a therapist in Nigeria.

Which professional you actually need, where to find them, how to check they are qualified, and what it should cost.

Last reviewed 1 October 2026 by Be Empowered Network. This page is information, not medical advice.

Start here

Three things nobody explains

Most people searching for a therapist in Nigeria get stuck on the same three things: they do not know which kind of professional they need, they cannot tell who is actually qualified, and they have no idea what it should cost. This page answers those three, in that order.

If you are in crisis right now, do not work through this page. Call 112, or 0809 111 6264 to talk to someone.

Step one

Which professional do you actually need?

These titles get used interchangeably in Nigeria, and they are not the same thing.

Psychiatrist

A medical doctor who specialises in mental health. The only one of the three who can prescribe medication. This is who you need for psychosis, bipolar disorder, severe depression, or anything that may need medical treatment. Found mainly in federal neuropsychiatric hospitals and teaching hospitals.

Clinical or counselling psychologist

Trained in assessment and talking therapies such as CBT. Cannot prescribe. This is who most people mean when they say "therapist" — the right choice for anxiety, low mood, grief, trauma and relationship difficulty.

Counsellor or therapist

A broad title covering a range of training. Many are excellent. Because the title is loosely used, this is the one where you most need to ask what qualification the person holds and where they are registered.

Step two

Where to look

  • Federal neuropsychiatric hospitals. Yaba (Lagos), Aro (Abeokuta), Enugu, Kaduna, Maiduguri, Calabar, Benin City, Sokoto. Subsidised and staffed by psychiatrists. Usually the cheapest route to medical treatment.
  • Teaching hospital psychiatry departments. LUTH in Lagos and UCH in Ibadan both run outpatient psychiatry and clinical psychology services.
  • Online therapy platforms. Several Nigerian platforms match you with a therapist by video, voice or chat, usually cheaper than seeing someone in person. Check the therapist's registration before you book.
  • NGO referrals. Organisations like ours, MANI and She Writes Woman can often point you to someone they trust in your state.

One thing worth knowing before you plan: the great majority of Nigeria's psychiatrists practise in Lagos and Abuja. If you live elsewhere, online care is not a compromise — it is often the realistic option.

Step three

How to check someone is genuinely qualified

Anyone can print "therapist" on a card. Before you pay for a first session, ask:

  • "What is your qualification, and where did you train?" A real professional will answer this without hesitation or offence.
  • "Where are you registered?" A psychiatrist is a medical doctor and should be registered with the Medical and Dental Council of Nigeria. Psychologists and counsellors should be able to name their professional body.
  • "What approach do you use, and have you worked with this before?"
  • "What do you charge, and do you offer a sliding scale?"

Leave if someone promises a cure, pressures you into a long package upfront, tells you to stop prescribed medication, or treats your faith or your sexuality as the illness.

Step four

What therapy costs in Nigeria

From what people are actually quoted when we help them look, private therapy in Nigeria commonly runs from about ₦40,000 a session to ₦300,000 and beyond. Where you land in that range depends on who you are seeing and what you need — a counselling session with a generalist sits at the bottom of it, while a senior specialist, or complex work such as trauma or a full psychiatric assessment, sits at the top.

Two things follow from that, and we would rather say them plainly than pretend otherwise:

  • For most Nigerians, sustained private therapy is simply not affordable. A course of treatment is not one session. At these fees, a few months of weekly sessions costs more than many people earn in that time.
  • That is an access problem, not a personal failing. If you cannot pay this, you have not fallen at the first hurdle. You are in the majority.

What brings the cost down:

  • Public hospital clinics. Federal neuropsychiatric and teaching hospitals are subsidised and remain by far the cheapest route to a psychiatrist and to medication.
  • Asking about a sliding scale. Some therapists keep a small number of reduced-fee places and do not advertise them. You have to ask.
  • Group work. Group therapy costs a fraction of one-to-one sessions.
  • Free peer support alongside it. Circles cost nothing and can carry you between sessions, or while you are saving for the first one.

Be careful with the fee figures published online. Several widely-shared articles still quote ranges from a few thousand naira upwards; those numbers have not kept pace with what clinicians in Nigeria actually charge now, and planning around them will leave you short.

Common questions

Questions people ask.

How much does therapy cost in Nigeria?

Private therapy in Nigeria commonly runs from about ₦40,000 a session to ₦300,000 and beyond, depending on who you see and what you need. Public psychiatric hospitals are far cheaper and remain the most affordable route to a psychiatrist and to medication. Always ask the fee before the first session, and ask whether there is a sliding scale.

What is the difference between a psychiatrist, a psychologist and a counsellor?

A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who can prescribe medication. A clinical or counselling psychologist is trained in talking therapies such as CBT but cannot prescribe. Counsellor and therapist are broader titles covering a range of training, so ask what qualification the person holds.

How do I know a therapist in Nigeria is qualified?

Ask what they trained in, where they trained and which professional body they are registered with. A psychiatrist should be registered with the Medical and Dental Council of Nigeria. A genuine professional will not be offended by the question.

Can I see a therapist online in Nigeria?

Yes, and for most people outside Lagos and Abuja it is the realistic option. Online sessions are typically cheaper because there is no clinic overhead. Check the therapist's registration before booking.

What if I cannot afford a therapist at all?

Start with what is free: helplines, peer support circles, student counselling units and NGO services. Public psychiatric hospitals are subsidised. Message us and a volunteer will help you work out what is realistic where you live.
