Depression: the signs, and where to get help in Nigeria.

What depression actually is, how it shows up here, what helps, and where to go next — whether or not you can pay.

Last reviewed 1 October 2026 by Be Empowered Network. This page is information, not medical advice.

In immediate danger, or someone's life is at risk Nigeria's national emergency number. Free from any phone, 24 hours a day. Call 112
Mentally Aware Nigeria Initiative (MANI) Peer-led support line for anyone struggling with their mental health. 0809 111 6264
Nigeria Suicide Prevention Initiative (NSPI) Counselling line run by a suicide prevention charity. 0806 210 6493

The basics

What depression actually is

Depression is not sadness, and it is not weakness. It is a health condition that affects how you feel, think, sleep, eat and function, and it does not lift because you decided to be strong about it.

In Nigeria it is often met with "pray harder", "other people have it worse", or "you are just lazy". None of that is treatment, and none of it is true. Depression responds to support and, where needed, to medical care — the same way any other condition does.

This page is information, not a diagnosis. Only a qualified professional can diagnose depression. If what you read here sounds like you, that is a reason to talk to someone, not a verdict.

Signs

What it can look like

Most people have some of these some of the time. What matters is how many, how long, and how much they are interfering with your life — roughly two weeks or more is the point at which it is worth talking to someone.

  • Low mood, emptiness or numbness that does not shift
  • Losing interest in things you used to enjoy, including people
  • Exhaustion that sleep does not fix
  • Sleeping far more or far less than usual
  • Appetite and weight changing noticeably in either direction
  • Struggling to concentrate, decide or remember
  • Feeling worthless, or guilty about things that are not your fault
  • Irritability and a short fuse — often how it shows up in men
  • Physical pain with no clear medical cause: headaches, body aches, stomach trouble
  • Thoughts that people would be better off without you

That last one is the one to act on immediately. If you are having thoughts of ending your life, call 112, or 0809 111 6264 to talk to a person now. Those thoughts are a symptom, and they are treatable.

Context

How depression shows up in Nigeria

Depression does not always present as "I feel sad" here. It is common for it to arrive as the body complaining — persistent headaches, back pain, stomach problems — and for people to move between hospitals looking for a physical cause that tests keep failing to find.

It is also common for it to be understood as a spiritual problem first. Faith can be a genuine support, and many Nigerians draw real strength from it. The harm comes when it replaces treatment: when someone is told their medication shows weak faith, or that a deliverance session is the whole answer. You are allowed to do both.

And stigma keeps people quiet. Fear of being called mad, of being passed over at work, of the family name — so people carry it silently for years. The silence is not evidence that nobody else has this. It is evidence of how good people have become at hiding it.

What helps

What actually helps

  • Telling one person the true version. Not the managed version. One honest conversation changes more than months of coping alone.
  • Talking therapy. CBT and related approaches have strong evidence behind them for depression, and work on the link between what you think, how you feel and what you do.
  • Medication, where a doctor recommends it. Antidepressants are not happy pills and they are not addictive in the way people fear. For moderate to severe depression they can be what makes everything else possible. That is a conversation for a psychiatrist.
  • Peer support. Sitting with people who recognise what you are describing removes the specific loneliness of thinking you are the only one.
  • Sleep, food, movement and sunlight. Not a cure, and being told to "just exercise" when you are depressed is infuriating. But they genuinely affect the floor you are working from.
  • Lowering the bar, deliberately. On a bad day, a shower and one meal is a complete day. Treating that as failure adds guilt to an illness that already manufactures guilt.

Next steps

Where to get help in Nigeria

  • Free and immediate: MANI on 0809 111 6264, NSPI on 0806 210 6493.
  • Free and ongoing: our peer support circles, online and in person, no diagnosis needed.
  • Medical care: a federal neuropsychiatric hospital or a teaching hospital psychiatry department — listed by state in our directory.
  • Private therapy: see how to find a therapist in Nigeria, including what it should cost.
  • If money is the obstacle: everything that is genuinely free.

If you are not ready to speak to a person yet, you can talk to Bee, our CBT companion, anywhere on this site. It is not a therapist and it is not a crisis service, but it will walk you through a tool and show you where to go next.

Supporting others

If it is someone you love

You do not need the right words. Ask directly — "you have not seemed yourself, how are you really?" — and then let them finish without fixing, correcting or quoting scripture at them.

If they mention not wanting to be here, take it seriously and ask about it plainly. Asking someone whether they are thinking about suicide does not put the idea in their head; it is one of the most useful things you can do. Stay with them, help them reach a helpline or a hospital, and do not leave them alone with the means to harm themselves.

Offer something concrete rather than "let me know if you need anything": sit with them while they make the call, or go with them to the first appointment. And look after yourself too — you can talk to us as well.

Common questions

Questions people ask.

How do I know if I am depressed or just going through a hard time?

The usual markers are duration and interference: symptoms most of the day, most days, for two weeks or more, and getting in the way of work, study, relationships or looking after yourself. A hard time usually lifts when the circumstances change; depression often does not. Only a qualified professional can diagnose it, so if you are asking the question it is worth talking to someone.

Is depression a spiritual problem?

Faith is a real source of strength for many Nigerians and it can sit alongside treatment. The harm comes when it replaces treatment — for example being told that taking medication shows weak faith. You are allowed to pray and to see a doctor.

Are antidepressants addictive?

Antidepressants are not addictive in the way the word is usually meant. They can cause withdrawal effects if stopped suddenly, which is why you reduce them with a doctor's guidance rather than on your own. Whether you need them at all is a conversation for a psychiatrist.

Can depression be treated for free in Nigeria?

Helplines and peer support circles are free. Public psychiatric hospitals are subsidised rather than free, and are the most affordable route to a psychiatrist and to medication. See our guide to free mental health help in Nigeria for the full list.

What do I do if someone tells me they want to die?

Take it seriously, stay with them, and ask directly — asking does not plant the idea. Call 112 if there is immediate danger, or MANI on 0809 111 6264 or NSPI on 0806 210 6493 to speak to a trained person. Do not leave them alone with the means to harm themselves.
